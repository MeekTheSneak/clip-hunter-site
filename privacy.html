Privacy policy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Clip Hunter is a personal tool. It runs only on its owner's own computer, is used only by that owner, and has no servers of its own. This page explains what it does with Google, YouTube and Meta data.

YouTube API Services

Clip Hunter uses YouTube API Services. By using Clip Hunter, you agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's handling of data is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

What Clip Hunter accesses

When the owner signs in with their Google account, Clip Hunter asks for permission to:

  • Upload videos (youtube.upload): to post finished clips to the signed-in user's own YouTube channel.
  • View YouTube data (youtube.readonly): to check the status of those uploads (processed, restricted or rejected), read their view counts, and look up which public Shorts about the same streamers perform well.
  • View YouTube Analytics (yt-analytics.readonly): to read how long viewers watch that channel's own videos.

When the owner connects Facebook, Clip Hunter asks Meta for permission to list the owner's Pages, post Reels to the owner's own Facebook Page, and publish Reels to the Instagram account linked to it.

It does not collect personal data about anyone, and it does not access any other account.

How the data is used

Only to post clips to the owner's own accounts and to measure how those clips perform, so the tool can make better clips. Clip Hunter does not use this data for advertising, does not sell it, and does not share or transfer it to anyone.

Clip Hunter's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Where it is kept, and for how long

Sign-in tokens, upload results and statistics stay on the owner's computer, in Clip Hunter's own folder. Nothing is sent to any server run by Clip Hunter, because there is none. Clip Hunter talks only to Google's, Meta's and Twitch's own services.

Tokens are kept while the owner uses Clip Hunter and are deleted within 30 days after access is removed or on request. Statistics about the owner's own videos are kept to improve the clips until the owner deletes them.

Twitch data

Clip Hunter reads public Twitch information: broadcasts, their public chat replay, and clips viewers made. It does not sign in to Twitch and does not access any private Twitch data.

Removing access and deleting data

  • Google and YouTube: remove access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Facebook and Instagram: remove the app under Facebook Settings, Business integrations.
  • Delete everything Clip Hunter stored by deleting its folder on the owner's computer, or ask via the contact link below.
