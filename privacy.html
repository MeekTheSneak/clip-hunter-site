Privacy policy

Last updated 19 November 2026

Clip Hunter is a personal tool. It runs only on its owner's own computer, is used only by that owner, and has no servers of its own. This page explains what it does with Google and YouTube data.

What Clip Hunter accesses

When the owner signs in with their Google account, Clip Hunter asks for permission to:

  • Upload videos (YouTube Data API, youtube.upload): to publish finished clips to the signed-in user's own YouTube channel.
  • View YouTube data (youtube.readonly): to read the view counts of that channel's own videos and to look up which public Shorts perform well.
  • View YouTube Analytics (yt-analytics.readonly): to read how long viewers watch that channel's own videos.

How the data is used

Only to upload clips to the owner's channel and to measure how those clips perform, so the tool can make better clips. Clip Hunter does not use Google data for advertising, does not sell it, and does not transfer it to anyone.

Clip Hunter's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Where the data is kept

The Google sign-in token and the statistics stay on the owner's computer, in Clip Hunter's own folder. Nothing is sent to any server run by Clip Hunter, because there is none. Clip Hunter only talks to Google's and Twitch's own services.

Twitch data

Clip Hunter reads public Twitch information: broadcasts, their public chat replay, and clips viewers made. It does not sign in to Twitch and does not access any private Twitch data.

Removing access

The owner can remove Clip Hunter's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, and delete the saved sign-in by deleting Clip Hunter's folder.
